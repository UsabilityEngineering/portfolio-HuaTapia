# Emulation with a SteamDeck                                                      
                                                                            Joshua Tapia Almanza November 10, 2025

I use a [Value SteamDeck](https://store.steampowered.com/steamdeck/) for all my emulations of video games that I wish to play on the go or when I'm away from my computer. The SteamDeck is really promoted for the fact that it connects to your steam profile and library but while you are away from your computer allowing you access your library and download games that you have in your library as long as they are compatible with the SteamDeck, it also has a feature of going into a desktop mode where you can access the internet and from there the main reason I have a SteamDeck begins, since there is a desktop mode you can go to any website you want like any of desktop and download files that you want to really customize your SteamDeck and truly make it your own.

![Desktop](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/f3b23fea-3f93-4ccc-a698-ffc85890d32e)


Like mentioned before you can really customize your SteamDeck in any way you want and one of the many things you can do is emulate games that aren't normally accessible through a PC such as Gameboy or Gamecube games. So to really get started into emulating on the SteamDeck you first have to download [EmuDeck](https://www.emudeck.com/) a tool that already has multiple emulator platforms on there making the process easier and quicker instead of individually having to download all these platforms. Something to note though is the fact that these emulators are there built in but they don't have the BIOs file installed which is necessary to even launch games on them. Fortunately the EmuDeck creators have also made a [wiki](https://emudeck.github.io/cheat-sheet/) explaining what they are and where to even get the BIOs files.

![Installation](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/ff301226-4c66-46bd-bb91-79c5e91df6be)

After installing the emulators and getting their BIOs files that you want to play on you can now start looking for roms. Roms are copies of video games files with all their code intact so when you download these roms on to your device you have the whole game in that folder you just downloaded. Unfortunately on their own you can't just launch the game which is why you downloaded the emulator related to that game console.

Once you have the emulator and the rom files you now have to get those files from your PC to your SteamDeck.Now there are 2 ways of doing this process one is moving those roms onto an external drive and then moving them into the Emulation folder and them roms. Another way is to connect an HDMI cable to a docked SteamDeck to your pc and then just move the folders that way. Personally I prefer the 1st option just because the only thing I have to worry about connecting anything is the flashdrive I use for them.
![Roms Folder](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/83fb8d08-d1d8-4feb-a9bb-368fcae371a4)

After you do get the roms into their respective folders you then launch Emudeck in desktop mode where you will then click on Steam ROM Manager and click on Add Games after that it will take you to a layout of the avaliable games you installed then you click Save to Steam 

Once the prompt "Done Adding/Removing Entries" pops up you are good to go, you can close everything and get out of Desktop mode. Once back into your normal home screen you'll see a new tab called "Non-Steam" and that is where all the roms you installed will be and now you can play video games you haven't played since you were a kid again and start feeling nostalgic.
